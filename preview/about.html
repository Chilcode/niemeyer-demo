The story

I spent almost ten years as a personal trainer. I loved it, and I built something I was proud of. Then my body stopped cooperating.

I was diagnosed with ulcerative colitis and spent more than two years trying to manage it. Medication, iron infusions, steroids in the ER. None of it worked, and I kept losing weight and energy.

In May of 2022, I had my colon removed. That was the first of three surgeries spread across nine months. My daughter Cleo was born three weeks after the first one.

In the middle of all that, I realized I couldn't count on my body the way I always had. Personal training is a job you do on your feet. With a baby coming and no income if I wasn't in the gym, I had to make a move.

So I studied for my real estate license from a hospital bed.

Licensed in January 2023. Full time by that October. And honestly? It felt surprisingly familiar, because both jobs are really about helping people get to a place they couldn't quite see for themselves yet.

That's my story, and it's the reason I show up the way I do for every family I work with.

What ten years of training taught me about this job

Nobody ever got in shape because a trainer yelled at them. They got there because somebody listened, explained what actually mattered, and stayed patient while they did the work.

Real estate is the same, and the three things I learned carry straight over.

Listen first.

Most people already know more about their situation than they think. My job is to hear it before I start talking.

Educate without an agenda.

In the gym I taught the basics, not the shortcuts. Here that means showing you real numbers on your real scenario, even when they point away from a deal.

Never push.

Nobody gets pressured into the right house, same as nobody gets pressured into a habit that lasts. If the math says wait, I'll tell you to wait.

I'm not the agent who talks you into anything. I'm the one who makes sure you understand what you're deciding.

This is home

I've never lived anywhere else, and I don't plan to.

That means when you ask me about a neighborhood, you're not getting a Google search read back to you. I know which commutes look fine on a map and turn brutal at 5pm. I know which streets take on water when it really comes down. I know which subdivisions have the HOA that actually maintains things and which ones don't.

I also know the parts that have nothing to do with real estate. Where to take the girls on a Saturday when everyone's restless. Which local spots are worth the drive and which ones are just close. The stuff you only pick up by living somewhere for thirty years.

Liberty, Kearney, Gladstone, Parkville, Smithville, and everywhere in between. This isn't a territory I picked. It's just where I'm from.

Outside of work

My wife Kelsey and I have two young daughters, Cleo and Vera. Most of my time outside this job belongs to them, which right now means a lot of early mornings and not much sleep.

I still work out, though I'll be honest, staying consistent is harder now that the gym isn't where I go to work. Turns out the advice I gave clients for ten years applies to me too: the answer is almost never something new, it's almost always getting back to the basics.

And I'm around. If you see me at an open house, a ball field, or the grocery store, say hi.
